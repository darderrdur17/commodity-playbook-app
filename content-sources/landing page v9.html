Free Sample · Case C
COVID Contango — Singapore's Anchorage Fills
April–June 2020 · Crude oil · Trading & shipping
Here's the full shape of one case study, free — so you can see exactly how the library is built before you unlock the rest.
1 · The Signal
On 20 April 2020, WTI front-month settled at −$37.63/bbl — negative for the first time in history. Brent's prompt-versus-six-month spread (M1/M6) gapped out to roughly $18/bbl: a textbook contango, where a barrel for later delivery is worth far more than one today.
2 · The Read
A contango that steep pays you to wait. If the gap between buying now and selling forward beats the cost of storing the barrels, storage itself becomes the trade. With onshore tanks brimming, the next option was floating storage — chartering tankers simply to sit and hold crude.
3 · The Evidence
AIS vessel-tracking showed VLCCs slowing and anchoring off Singapore and the Strait of Malacca before any official inventory report confirmed the build. The ships were the signal; the data caught up weeks later.
The carry, simplified
Contango captured (M1 → M6)≈ +$18.00 /bbl
Storage + freight (VLCC hire)– est. $7–9 /bbl
Financing + insurance– est. $2–3 /bbl
Indicative net carry≈ +$6–9 /bbl
Illustrative figures — desks repriced this daily against live charter rates.
4 · The Decision
Desks that locked storage early — before charter rates spiked on the very same demand — captured the carry. As tanks and tankers filled and OPEC+ cuts took hold, the spread normalised over the following weeks and the window closed. Timing, not direction, made the money.

The Problem

Your Buyers Know When You Don't Get It.

Commodity trading firms — oil majors, independent trading houses, LNG portfolios, market intelligence providers — buy from people who understand their business. Most vendors don't. That's why deals stall, champions go quiet, and pilots never convert.

You're Pitching to People Who Think in Barrels

Traders don't think in annual recurring revenue, user seats, or implementation timelines. They think in cargo positions, freight rates, and margin at risk. If your discovery call sounds like a software demo instead of a market conversation, you've already lost them.

Your Champion Can't Sell You Internally

Even when your champion sees the value, they struggle to articulate it to a trading desk in commercial terms. They need to explain how your solution maps to their P&L, their risk exposure, or their operational workflow — and most vendors don't give them the language to do it.

You Can't Differentiate on Product Alone

Your competitors have similar feature sets. The vendor who wins is the one who understands the buyer's commercial context deeply enough to position their solution as the answer to a specific, felt problem — not just another capability on a slide.
